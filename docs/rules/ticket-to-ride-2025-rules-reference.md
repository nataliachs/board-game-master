# Ticket to Ride (2025 Edition) — Rules Reference

This is an original plain-language summary of the base game rules, written for Board Game Master.
It does not reproduce the official rules booklet by Days of Wonder. It covers the base game on the
map of North America for 2 to 5 players. It does not cover expansions or other maps, such as
Europe, and it does not give strategy advice.

## Overview of the game

Ticket to Ride is a train game played on a map of North America. Players collect colored train
cards and spend them to claim railway routes between neighboring cities. A game takes about 60
minutes and is for 2 to 5 players.

## Object of the game

The goal is to finish with the most points. Players score points in three ways: by claiming
routes, by completing the destination tickets they kept, and by having the longest continuous
path of trains, which earns a bonus. Destination tickets that a player kept but did not complete
subtract points at the end of the game.

## Setup: board, trains and scoring markers

Place the board with the map of North America in the middle of the table. Each player chooses a
color, takes exactly 45 plastic trains of that color and the scoring marker of the same color.
The box contains a few spare trains of each color, so count exactly 45 trains per player. Every
scoring marker starts on the 0 space of the scoring track around the edge of the board.

## Setup: train cards and the face-up display

Shuffle the train cards and deal 4 train cards to each player as their starting hand. Place the
rest of the train card deck beside the board and turn the top 5 cards face up next to it. These 5
face-up cards are the card display. If 3 or more of the 5 face-up cards are locomotives, discard
all 5 and turn up 5 new cards. Place the longest path bonus card face up beside the board.

## Setup: starting destination tickets

Shuffle the destination tickets and deal 4 destination tickets to each player. Each player
secretly keeps at least 2 of their starting tickets and may keep 3 or all 4. Shuffle the returned
tickets together and put them at the bottom of the destination ticket deck, then place the deck
beside the board. Keep your destination tickets hidden from other players until the end of the
game.

## Who goes first and turn order

Choose a first player in any way you like. Play then continues clockwise around the table.

## Your turn: one of three actions

On your turn you must do exactly one of these three actions, and only one:

1. Draw train cards.
2. Claim one route.
3. Draw destination tickets.

You cannot combine actions in the same turn. For example, you cannot draw train cards and then
claim a route on the same turn. Most players draw cards for a few turns before they have enough
cards to claim a long route.

## Drawing train cards

When you choose to draw train cards, you take 2 train cards. Each card can be taken from the 5
face-up cards or drawn blind from the top of the deck, in any combination: two face-up cards, two
cards from the deck, or one of each. When you take a face-up card, immediately refill that empty
spot with the top card of the deck before taking your second card. There is no hand limit: you
may hold any number of train cards. When the train card deck runs out, shuffle the discarded
train cards to form a new deck.

## Locomotives (wild cards)

Locomotives are wild train cards. When claiming a route, a locomotive counts as a card of any
color, and you can mix locomotives with colored cards.

Taking a face-up locomotive from the display is special. A face-up locomotive can only be taken
as your first card of the turn, and if you take it, you take no second card that turn. You can
never take a face-up locomotive as your second card, including a locomotive that was just turned
up when the display was refilled.

A locomotive drawn blind from the top of the deck counts like any other single card, so you still
draw your second card normally.

If 3 of the 5 face-up cards are ever locomotives at the same time, discard all 5 face-up cards and
turn up 5 new cards from the deck.

## Claiming a route

A route is the row of colored spaces between two neighboring cities on the map. To claim a route,
play a set of train cards equal to the number of spaces in that route. All cards in the set must
match the color of the route; locomotives can stand in for any color. Then place one of your
plastic trains on each space of the route and discard the cards you played.

You claim the whole route at once; you cannot claim part of a route. You can claim only one route
per turn. A route you claim does not need to connect to routes you already own. Once a route is
claimed by any player, no other player can claim that same route.

## Gray routes

Gray routes have no color of their own. To claim a gray route, play a set of cards that are all
the same color, of any one color you choose, with as many cards as the route has spaces.
Locomotives can be included in the set. You cannot mix two different colors in one gray route.

## Route scoring

Score points as soon as you claim a route by moving your scoring marker forward on the scoring
track. The points depend on the length of the route:

| Route length (spaces) | Points |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 7 |
| 5 | 10 |
| 6 | 15 |

Longer routes are worth much more per train than short ones.

## Double routes

Some pairs of cities are joined by two parallel routes, called a double route. A single player can
never claim both routes of the same double route.

In games with 2 or 3 players, only one route of each double route can be used. Once one of the two
parallel routes is claimed, the other route is closed for the rest of the game and no one can
claim it. In games with 4 or 5 players, both routes of a double route can be used, but by two
different players.

## Drawing destination tickets

When you choose to draw destination tickets, draw 3 tickets from the top of the destination
ticket deck. If fewer than 3 tickets remain, draw all that remain. You must keep at least 1 of
the tickets you drew, and you may keep 2 or all 3. Return the tickets you do not keep to the
bottom of the deck.

You can only discard tickets you have just drawn this turn. Tickets you kept earlier stay in your
hand for the rest of the game and cannot be discarded. Keep all your tickets secret until the end
of the game.

## Destination tickets and completing them

Each destination ticket names two cities and shows a point value. A ticket is complete when an
unbroken line of your own trains connects the two cities printed on it. The connection can pass
through other cities. At the end of the game, a completed ticket adds its point value to your
score, and an incomplete ticket subtracts its point value from your score.

## How the game ends

The end of the game is triggered when a player finishes their turn with 2 or fewer trains left in
their supply. Then every player, including the player who triggered the end, takes one final turn.
After those final turns the game ends and players calculate final scores.

## Final scoring

At the end of the game:

1. Recount the route points to check for scoring mistakes during play.
2. Every player reveals their destination tickets. Add the value of each completed ticket and
   subtract the value of each incomplete ticket.
3. Award the longest path bonus of 10 points to the player with the longest continuous path of
   trains in their own color.

The player with the highest total score wins.

## Longest path bonus

The longest path bonus card is worth 10 points. It goes to the player with the longest continuous
path made of their own trains, counted in train spaces. A path can loop back on itself and can
pass through the same city more than once, but each individual train can be counted only once.
If several players tie for the longest path, each tied player scores the 10-point bonus.

## Breaking a tie

If two or more players tie for the highest total score, the tied player who completed the most
destination tickets wins. If they are still tied, the tied player who has the longest path bonus
wins. If they are still tied, they share the victory.
