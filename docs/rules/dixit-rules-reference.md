# Dixit (2021 Edition) — Rules Reference

This is an original plain-language summary of the base game rules, written for Board Game Master.
It does not reproduce the official rulebook by Libellud. It covers the base game for 3 to 8
players. It does not cover the expansions, and it does not give strategy advice beyond the
storyteller guidance in the rules.

## Overview of the game

Dixit is a guessing game played with 84 illustrated picture cards. Each round, one player is the
storyteller. The storyteller gives a clue about one of their cards, the other players add cards
from their own hands that fit the clue, and then everyone tries to find the storyteller's card
among them. The game is for 3 to 8 players.

## Object of the game

The goal is to have the most points when the game ends. The storyteller scores when some players,
but not all of them, find the storyteller's card. The other players score by finding the
storyteller's card and by attracting votes to their own decoy cards. The game ends when one or
more players reach or pass 30 points.

## Components

The base game contains a rulebook, 84 Dixit picture cards, 8 voting dials, 8 wooden rabbit pawns,
and a game board. The board has a scoring track, 8 numbered card slots, and a players' aid that
summarizes how scoring works.

## Setup

1. Place the board in the middle of the table.
2. Each player chooses a color and takes the voting dial and the rabbit pawn of that color.
3. Each player puts their rabbit pawn on the starting space of the scoring track. The rabbit
   shows that player's score.
4. Shuffle all 84 cards and deal 6 cards face down to each player. With 3 players, deal 7 cards
   each instead (see Playing with 3 players).
5. Put the remaining cards face down as the draw pile.

Players keep the cards in their hand secret.

## Choosing the first storyteller

After setup, the first player who thinks of a clue becomes the storyteller for the first round.
After that, the role of storyteller passes to the left each round.

## A round of play

Every round has the same order: the storyteller gives a clue, every other player picks a matching
card, the storyteller lays out all the cards, the other players vote, the round is scored, and
then everyone refills their hand.

## The storyteller's clue

The storyteller looks at the 6 cards in their hand and secretly chooses one that inspires them,
without showing it. They say a clue about that card out loud. The clue can be one word, a phrase
or a sentence of any length. It can be made up or borrowed from an existing work such as a poem,
song, film or proverb. The clue can also be sung, mimed, or be a sound or onomatopoeia.

## Giving a good clue

The storyteller scores only if at least one player, but not every player, finds their card. A
clue that is too easy, such as one that simply describes the picture, lets everyone find the
card. A clue that is too difficult, such as one that is too abstract or too personal, lets
nobody find it. In both cases the storyteller scores nothing, so a good clue strikes a balance
between the two.

## Other players choose a card

After hearing the clue, each other player chooses the card from their own hand of 6 that they
think best matches the clue. Each player secretly gives that card face down to the storyteller.
The storyteller shuffles all the cards they received together with their own chosen card.

## Laying out the cards

The storyteller places the shuffled cards face up in random order next to the numbered card slots
on the board, one card per slot, with the slot numbers left visible. For example, with 6 players
there are 6 cards, placed in slots 1 to 6.

## Voting

The other players try to find the storyteller's card. The storyteller does not vote. Each other
player takes their voting dial and secretly turns it to the number of the card they believe is
the storyteller's card. A player may not vote for their own card. When everyone has voted, all
players reveal their voting dials at the same time and place them on the cards they chose.

## Scoring a round

The storyteller reveals which card is theirs and counts the votes on it.

- **Everyone found the storyteller's card, or nobody did:** the storyteller scores 0 points and
  every other player scores 2 points.
- **Some players found it, but not all of them:** the storyteller scores 3 points, each player who
  voted for the storyteller's card scores 3 points, and the players who did not find it score
  0 points for their vote.

In addition, every player except the storyteller scores 1 bonus point for each vote that their
own card received. Players move their rabbit pawn one space along the scoring track for each
point they score.

## Scoring example

In a 6-player game, 2 of the 5 voters find the storyteller's card. The
storyteller scores 3 points and the 2 players who found the card score 3 points each. The other
3 voters score nothing for their vote. One voter picked another player's card, so that card's
owner scores 1 bonus point. Two voters picked the card of one of the players who found the
storyteller's card, so that player scores 2 bonus points on top of their 3, for 5 points in total.

## End of the round

All the cards used during the round are placed face up in a discard pile away from the game. Each
player draws 1 card from the draw pile so that they have 6 cards again. If there are not enough
cards left in the draw pile for every player, shuffle the remaining cards together with the
discard pile to form a new draw pile. The player to the left of the storyteller becomes the
storyteller for the next round.

## End of the game

If, at the end of a round, one or more players have reached or passed 30 points on the scoring
track, the game ends immediately. The player with the most points wins. If several players tie
for the most points, they share the victory.

## Playing with 3 players

With exactly 3 players, a few rules change:

- Each player holds 7 cards instead of 6.
- When choosing cards for the clue, each player except the storyteller selects 2 cards instead
  of 1, so 5 cards are laid out, including the storyteller's card.
- During scoring, each player except the storyteller still scores 1 bonus point for each vote
  received on either of their own cards.
- At the end of the round, players draw 2 cards instead of 1 to refill their hand.

All other rules stay the same.
