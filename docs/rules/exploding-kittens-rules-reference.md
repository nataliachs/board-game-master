# Exploding Kittens (Original Edition) — Rules Reference

This is an original plain-language summary of the base game rules, written for Board Game Master.
It does not reproduce the official rules sheet by Exploding Kittens. It covers the Original
Edition base game for 2 to 5 players with 56 cards, and gives no strategy advice beyond the tips
in the rules.

## Overview of the game

Exploding Kittens is a card game for 2 to 5 players. The deck contains a few Exploding Kitten
cards. Players take turns drawing cards from a face-down draw pile. A player who draws an
Exploding Kitten explodes and is out of the game, unless they can play a Defuse. All the other
cards are tools that help you avoid drawing an Exploding Kitten or push the danger onto other
players.

## Object of the game and how to win

The goal is to be the last player who has not exploded. Every time a player draws an Exploding
Kitten without a Defuse to save them, they are out. The game continues until only 1 player is
left, and that player wins.

## Components

The game has 56 cards:

- Exploding Kitten: 4 cards
- Defuse: 6 cards
- Attack: 4 cards
- Favor: 4 cards
- Nope: 5 cards
- Shuffle: 4 cards
- Skip: 4 cards
- See the Future: 5 cards
- Cat Cards: 4 of each kind

## Setup

1. Take the 4 Exploding Kittens out of the deck and set them aside.
2. Take the 6 Defuse cards out of the deck and deal 1 Defuse to each player.
3. Shuffle the rest of the deck and deal 7 cards face down to each player. Each player now has
   8 cards: 7 cards plus 1 Defuse. Look at your cards but keep them secret.
4. Shuffle 2 of the leftover Defuse cards back into the deck and remove any other leftover
   Defuses from the game. With 5 players there is only 1 leftover Defuse, so shuffle just that
   one back in.
5. Put Exploding Kittens back into the deck: 1 fewer than the number of players (for example, 3
   with 4 players, 2 with 3 players). Remove the extra Exploding Kittens from the game.
6. Shuffle the deck and place it face down in the middle of the table as the draw pile. Leave
   space next to it for a discard pile.
7. Choose who goes first.

## Faster variant for 2 or 3 players

This variant is recommended for 2 and 3 players. During setup, before putting the Exploding
Kittens back in, remove about one third of the deck at random without looking at it. Then
shuffle the correct number of Exploding Kittens into the remaining draw pile and start the game.

## Your turn: play or pass, then draw

On your turn, take these steps:

1. Play or pass. You may play as many cards from your hand as you like, one after another, by
   putting each one face up on the discard pile and doing what it says. You may also play no
   cards at all (pass).
2. Draw. End your turn by drawing the top card of the draw pile into your hand.

In short: play or pass, then draw. Play then continues clockwise to the next player.

Some cards (Attack and Skip) end your turn without drawing a card.

## Drawing an Exploding Kitten

If you draw an Exploding Kitten, you must show it at once. Unless you play a Defuse, you explode
and are out of the game. Put the Exploding Kitten face up in front of you so everyone can see you
are out, and put the rest of your cards face down in front of you.

## Defuse

If you draw an Exploding Kitten, you can play a Defuse instead of exploding. Put the Defuse on the
discard pile. Then, without looking at or rearranging the other cards, secretly put the Exploding
Kitten back into the draw pile at any position you choose, for example on top to threaten the
next player. You may hold the draw pile under the table so no one sees where you put it. Your turn
ends after you play a Defuse.

## Attack

End your turn without drawing a card, and the next player must take 2 turns in a row.

If the player who was attacked plays an Attack on one of their turns, the attacks stack: their
remaining turns pass to the next player, who must take those turns plus 2 more. For example, if
the attacked player plays an Attack straight away, the next player must take 4 turns. If the
attacked player finishes 1 turn and plays an Attack on their second turn, the next player must
take 3 turns.

## Skip

End your turn at once without drawing a card. If you play a Skip when you have to take 2 turns
because of an Attack, it ends only 1 of those turns; you would need 2 Skips to end both.

## Favor

Choose another player. That player must give you 1 card from their hand, and they choose which
card to give.

## Nope

A Nope stops any action except an Exploding Kitten or a Defuse. Treat the card that was Noped as
if it had never been played; it stays in the discard pile and is lost.

You can play a Nope at any time before an action has started, even when it is not your turn. You
can play a Nope on a Special Combo. You can also play a Nope on another Nope to cancel it, which
lets the original action happen ("Yup"), and so on.

## Shuffle

Shuffle the draw pile at random so that no one knows the order of the cards.

## See the Future

Look at the top 3 cards of the draw pile in secret, then put them back in the same order. Do not
show them to the other players.

## Cat Cards

Cat Cards do nothing on their own. If you play 2 matching Cat Cards together as a pair, you steal
a random card from any other player. Cat Cards can also be used in Special Combos.

## Special Combos

These combos are suggested once you have played your first game. When you play cards as a Special
Combo, ignore the instructions printed on them.

- Two of a kind: play any 2 cards with the same title (not only Cat Cards, but also, for example,
  2 Shuffle or 2 Attack cards) to steal a random card from another player.
- Three of a kind: play any 3 cards with the same title and name a card. If the chosen player has
  that card, they must give it to you. If not, you get nothing.

## Hand size and running out of cards

There is no maximum or minimum hand size. If your hand is empty, nothing special happens; you
still draw a card at the end of your next turn.

The draw pile never runs out, because the deck holds enough Exploding Kittens to knock out every
player except one.

## How the game ends

The game ends when all players but one have exploded. The remaining player wins the game.

## Tips from the rules

- Saving your cards early in the game, while the chance of exploding is low, is generally a good
  idea.
- You can count the cards left in the draw pile to work out your chances of drawing an Exploding
  Kitten.
