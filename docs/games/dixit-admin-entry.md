**Short description**

```
Tell a story with a single clue. Players guess which dreamlike picture is yours, so make it not too easy and not too hard.
```

## 2. Setup steps

Fill one card per step, in this order. Leave **Key** empty.

### Step 1 — Place the board
**Instruction**
```
Put the board in the middle of the table. It has the scoring track around the edge, numbered card slots, and a reminder of how scoring works.
```

### Step 2 — Take a dial and a rabbit
**Instruction**
```
Each player picks a color and takes the voting dial and the rabbit pawn of that color.
```

### Step 3 — Rabbits on the start
**Instruction**
```
Put every rabbit on the starting space of the scoring track. Your rabbit shows your score during the game.
```

### Step 4 — Deal the cards
**Instruction**
```
Shuffle all 84 picture cards and deal 6 face down to each player. With exactly 3 players, deal 7 each instead.
```
**Warnings**
```
Keep your cards hidden from everyone else.
```

### Step 5 — Make the draw pile
**Instruction**
```
Put the rest of the cards face down beside the board as the draw pile. Leave space next to it for a discard pile.
```

### Step 6 — Ready to play
**Instruction**
```
Check that everyone has a dial, a rabbit on the start space and a full hand. The first player to think of a clue becomes the first storyteller. Complete setup to return to your lesson.
```
**Notes**
```
Completion records your confirmation; the application cannot inspect the physical table.
```

## 3. Lessons

Fill one card per lesson, in this order. Leave **Key** empty. **Shown in** says which boxes to
tick. Pick **Starts setup** only on lesson 3.

### Lesson 1 — Welcome to Dixit
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
Dixit is a guessing game played with dreamlike picture cards. Each round, one player is the storyteller: they give a clue about one of their cards, and everyone else tries to spot it among several pictures. We will learn one step of a round at a time.
```

### Lesson 2 — Your goal
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
Score the most points. You score by finding the storyteller's card, by fooling other players into voting for your card, and, when you are the storyteller, by giving a clue that some players solve but not all of them. The game ends at the end of a round in which someone reaches 30 points.
```

### Lesson 3 — Prepare the table
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed · **Starts setup**

**Explanation**
```
Choose Continue to start guided setup. When the table is ready, return here to learn how a round works.
```

### Lesson 4 — The storyteller gives a clue
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
The storyteller secretly picks one card from their hand and says a clue about it out loud. The clue can be a single word, a sentence, a line from a song or film, a sound, or even a mime. The storyteller keeps the chosen card face down.
```

### Lesson 5 — Everyone picks a matching card
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
Every other player chooses the card from their own hand that best fits the clue and hands it face down to the storyteller. The storyteller shuffles these cards together with their own card, without anyone seeing which is which.
```

**Tips**
```
Pick a card that could fool the others: you score a bonus for every vote your card attracts.
```

### Lesson 6 — Laying out the cards
Shown in: Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
The storyteller lays the shuffled cards face up in random order beside the numbered slots on the board, one card per slot, starting at 1. Keep the slot numbers visible: players vote by number.
```

### Lesson 7 — Voting
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
The storyteller lays the shuffled cards face up beside the numbered slots on the board. Everyone except the storyteller then secretly turns their voting dial to the number of the card they think belongs to the storyteller. You may not vote for your own card. When everyone is ready, reveal all the dials at the same time and place each one on the card it shows.
```

### Lesson 8 — Scoring a round
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
The storyteller reveals their card. If every other player found it, or nobody did, the storyteller scores nothing and everyone else scores 2. Otherwise the storyteller scores 3, each player who found the card scores 3, and the rest score nothing. Then every player except the storyteller scores 1 extra point for each vote their own card received. Move your rabbit one space per point.
```

**Examples**
```
Six players: the storyteller's card gets 2 of the 5 votes. The storyteller and those 2 players each score 3. One player's decoy card got 2 votes, so that player also scores 2 bonus points.
```

### Lesson 9 — Giving a good clue
Shown in: Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
As storyteller you only score when some players find your card but not all of them. A clue that simply describes the picture is too easy; a clue that is too abstract or personal is too hard. Aim for something that points at your card while leaving room for other pictures to fit.
```

**Tips**
```
A feeling, a memory or a famous quote often works better than describing what is in the picture.
```

### Lesson 10 — End of the round
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
All cards played this round go face up into the discard pile. Everyone draws back up to a full hand. If the draw pile runs short, shuffle it together with the discard pile to make a new one. The player on the storyteller's left becomes the next storyteller.
```

### Lesson 11 — Winning the game
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
If anyone has reached or passed 30 points at the end of a round, the game ends straight away. The player with the most points wins. If players tie for the most points, they share the victory.
```

### Lesson 12 — Playing with three players
Shown in: Quick, Standard, Detailed

**Explanation**
```
With exactly 3 players, everyone holds 7 cards. The two players who are not the storyteller each give 2 cards instead of 1, so 5 cards are laid out. They still score 1 bonus point for each vote on either of their cards, and they draw 2 cards at the end of the round. Everything else is unchanged.
```

### Lesson 13 — A full round, step by step
Shown in: Detailed

**Explanation**
```
Here is one complete round with 5 players. Ana is the storyteller. She picks a card showing a lighthouse in a storm and says "Waiting for someone". Ben, Cara, Dev and Eli each give her one card from their hands that fits the clue, and she shuffles all 5 cards and lays them beside slots 1 to 5. Ben and Cara vote for Ana's card. Dev and Eli are fooled and both vote for Ben's card. Some players found Ana's card but not all, so Ana scores 3, Ben and Cara score 3 each, and Dev and Eli score nothing for their votes. Ben also scores 2 bonus points for the 2 votes on his card, so his round total is 5.
```

**Examples**
```
Same round, but nobody finds Ana's card: Ana scores 0 and the other four score 2 each, plus 1 bonus point for each vote their own card received.
```

### Lesson 14 — Clue ideas
Shown in: Detailed

**Explanation**
```
Imagine your card shows a small boat drifting under a huge moon. "A boat" is too easy, because everyone will find it. "Summer at my aunt's house" is too personal, because nobody can follow it. "Far from home" or "The quiet after the party" point at the feeling of the picture, so some players will find it and others will be drawn to different cards. You can also use a song lyric, a film title, a proverb, a sound or a gesture.
```

**Tips**
```
Before you say your clue, glance at the kinds of pictures in the game and ask yourself whether at least one other card could fit it.
```

### Lesson 15 — Common mistakes
Shown in: Detailed

**Explanation**
```
Watch out for these: you may never vote for your own card. The storyteller does not vote. Everyone reveals their dial at the same time, not one by one. The storyteller shuffles the cards before laying them out, so nobody can tell whose is whose. Refill your hand at the end of every round. Check for the end of the game only after the round is fully scored, and remember that tied leaders share the win. With 3 players, the other two players each give 2 cards, not 1.
```
